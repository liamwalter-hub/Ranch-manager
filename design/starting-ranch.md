# Ranch Manager — The Starting Ranch

_Companion to `design/game-concept-and-design.md` and `design/staff.md`. All names are fictional; real places (Billings, Miles City) are used only as geography._

_Figures are placeholders grounded in 2025–26 Montana market data (sources at the end). They will be tuned for gameplay._

---

## 1. Overview: Thistle Creek Ranch

- **Location:** central Montana, where the foothills meet the plains. Mountains to the west, open grassland to the east.
- **Nearest town:** Harlan Crossing (pop. ~900), 35 miles by gravel and highway. Billings is ~2.5 hours away.
- **Size:** ~6,000 deeded acres, plus a ~2,500-acre **state grazing lease**
- **Operation:** cow-calf with spring calving. Calves are sold in autumn at the sale barn.
- **Headline condition:** good bones, poor management. The land, water and location are excellent, but years of decline have left:
  - Overgrazed pasture and failing fences
  - An under-stocked herd with ageing bulls
  - Patchy records
  - Hidden liabilities

### Backstory and how the player bought it
- The ranch belonged to the **Calloway family** for three generations. Old Frank Calloway ran it alone after his wife died, and declined over the last decade. He sold down the herd during drought and let maintenance slide. His children live out of state and wanted a quick sale.
- **There were two bidders: the player and Summit Ridge Holdings** (see section 10). Summit Ridge offered more. The Calloways chose the player because Frank wanted Thistle Creek to stay a working cattle ranch. The locals know this, which gives the player some early goodwill and a reputation to live up to.
- **The player bought the ranch company, Thistle Creek Cattle Co. LLC, as a going concern,** rather than just the land. This is why:
  - The **staff come with it** on their existing terms, which supports the agreed "past probation" rule.
  - The company's **contracts, debts and liabilities come with it too.** Due diligence was rushed to beat the rival bid, so there are surprises (June's compliance thread).
- **Player background (flavour):** someone who made their money outside farming (sold a business, a city career). They paid for the company outright, so there's **no mortgage on the land**, but only modest working capital is left over.

## 2. Starting date & calendar

- **The game starts on 1 March:** late winter, snow on the ground, hay running low, and calving about to start.
- **Montana ranch year (drives event weighting):**

| Months | What's happening |
|---|---|
| Mar–Apr | **Calving.** Night checks, blizzards, sick calves, fatigue |
| May–Jun | **Branding,** vaccinations, getting cattle to grass, bull turnout, state lease paperwork |
| Jun–Sep | **Summer range** in the foothills (predators, water, fence riding) |
| Jul–Aug | **Haying** on the creek meadows (weather windows, equipment, overtime) |
| Aug–Sep | **Wildfire and drought** risk |
| Oct | **Weaning and shipping** calves: the main payday |
| Oct–Nov | Pregnancy checking, culling decisions, buying hay for winter |
| Dec–Feb | **Winter feeding,** storms, cash-flow squeeze, planning |

- **MVP season:** 1 March to mid-June turnout (calving and branding). That's about 3.5 real months at 1:1, so playtesting will need a debug speed setting as well as normal fast forward.

**Boundaries and neighbours on the map:**
- **North:** the **Kessler Ranch**, an old family outfit. Mostly friendly, but they compete for good staff (they may try to poach Rosa) and share Spring Gulch.
- **East:** land owned by **Summit Ridge Holdings**, the rival bidder (section 10).
- **West:** national forest (wildfire risk, grazing next to it, wildlife coming down).
- **South:** the county road to Harlan Crossing.

**Carrying capacity:** in this part of Montana, a cow and calf need roughly 20–30 acres a year depending on grass condition. With healthy pasture and the lease, the ranch could carry around **280–300 pairs**. At its current condition it's nearer **200**. Improving pasture is how the herd can grow.

## 6. Feed & water

- **Hay on hand at start:** around **90 tons**, which is not enough to get the herd to grass in mid-May, especially if April storms hit. Cows on full hay eat roughly 30 lb a day each.
  - **The first big decision:** buy hay now at high late-winter prices, turn out early and risk the pasture, or cut rations and risk cow condition at calving.
- **Hay production:** the creek meadows can produce roughly **600–800 tons** in a good year, if the headgate is fixed, irrigation water is available and the weather allows. Winter needs are roughly 2 tons per cow.
- **Water:** the irrigation **water right** on Thistle Creek is senior and valuable, but the paperwork is a mess.

## 7. Finances (starting position)

**Company:** Thistle Creek Cattle Co. LLC

| Item | Starting value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | ~$140,000 | Working capital left after the purchase |
| **Operating line of credit** | $200,000 limit, **$65,000 already drawn** | Inherited with the company. June uncovers a **covenant** (conditions) the player didn't know about, plus the renewal date |
| **Unpaid supplier invoices** | ~$18,000 (discovered over time) | Feed merchant, vet, fuel |
| **Overdue property tax** | ~$9,000 (discovered) | Penalties grow |
| **Lapsed insurance** | (discovered) | Uninsured until fixed; a disaster before then is very costly |
| **Land & assets (valuation)** | ~$7.5–8.5M | Land, herd, equipment. This is the "sell up" value. It rises with improvements and land prices, and falls with neglect |

### Rough annual picture at current state (placeholder)
**Income (mostly in October):**
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| ~150 calves sold at ~550 lb, ~$370/cwt | ~$300k |
| Cull cows | ~$35k |
| **Total** | **~$335k** |

**Costs (spread across the year):**
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Wages and staff costs | ~$160k |
| Bought-in hay and feed | ~$40k |
| Vet and medicine | ~$15k |
| Fuel and repairs | ~$40k |
| Property tax, insurance, state lease | ~$35k |
| Miscellaneous | ~$20k |
| **Total** | **~$310k** |

**Result:** roughly break-even before any investment. Cattle prices are at record highs, but the ranch is under-stocked and poorly run.

**The core money tension:** most income arrives in one hit in October, while costs run all year. Cash is tightest in late winter and early spring, which is exactly when the game starts.

**Fail state:** if cash runs out and the operating line is maxed or withdrawn (a breached covenant, or a failed renewal), the bank can call the debt. That leads to forced sales and, eventually, foreclosure.

**Levers to grow:**
- Rebuild pasture and grow the herd
- Better calf weights and documented vaccinations (higher price)
- Retain heifers to expand
- Sell hay
- Diversify later (guest ranch, hunting leases)
- Sell some land: this brings in cash but changes the ranch for good

## 10. Summit Ridge Holdings: an ongoing concern (AGREED)

**Not a major storyline or villain.** Summit Ridge is a constant background presence the player is always aware of, not a dramatic antagonist.

- **Who they are:** an out-of-state investment company buying Montana land for luxury homes and a private hunting retreat. Professional and polite, with deep pockets.
- **History:** they were the **rival bidder** for Thistle Creek and lost, even though they offered more. They then bought the neighbouring ranch to the east instead.
- **How they show up:** occasionally, and in ways that **nudge** other decisions rather than drive their own storyline:
  - **A standing offer:** they let it be known they'd still buy Thistle Creek. The offer **rises when the ranch is struggling** (they smell an opportunity) and is the natural buyer for the "sell up" option.
  - **Approaches for parts of the ranch:** the creek frontage, an easement, a slice of land, or the water right. These are a tempting cash injection, especially in a bad year.
  - **Labour market pressure:** they pay above local rates for caretakers and hunting guides, which raises wage expectations and poaching risk for staff.
  - **Neighbour friction:** a boundary fence, road use, their contractors, cattle straying onto their land, their hunting guests.
  - **Community effects:** rising local house prices, locals' feelings about outsiders, and how the town judges the player's dealings with them. Selling to Summit Ridge would cost a lot of community goodwill, given why the Calloways chose the player.
- **Frequency:** low. Roughly an occasional event every few weeks, more often if the ranch is in financial trouble.
- **MVP:** keep it minimal. One introductory event (a polite letter or visit reminding the player of their interest), plus the standing offer as a background figure.
